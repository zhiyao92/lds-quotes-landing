Prayer when life is unsettled

Room for prayer, even before everything is resolved.

A disagreement, a delayed answer and a crowded schedule each ask something different of us. This collection brings together an original reflection about praying for another person and two scripture-based reflections about waiting and stillness.

The readings do not prescribe a particular feeling or an immediate answer. They leave space to be honest about what is unresolved, to keep necessary boundaries and to find a pause that fits your circumstances. The scripture links open the full chapters so the short passages can be read in context.

Prayer and disagreement

A conversation ends, and you still disagree. Later, the other person’s name comes to mind while you are praying.

Following Christ can include asking for someone’s well-being without pretending their view has become yours. You might pray that they have support, clarity, or a safe journey home. Goodwill does not require a forced friendship or an unsafe conversation. It can begin without winning anything.

What would a sincere prayer for someone you disagree with include?

Hope, patience and prayer

LDS Quotes reflection

An unanswered message or a delayed decision can leave a day feeling unfinished. Romans 12:12 places hope alongside tribulation and ongoing prayer. The verse does not describe a trouble-free life; its encouragement makes room for difficulty that is still unresolved. Patience need not mean calling that difficulty small.

If you’d like, which word offers you something to consider today: hope, patience, or prayer?

Scripture + reflection · First shared 6 November 2026

A pause in an unsettled day

LDS Quotes reflection

A crowded schedule can leave little room to pause, especially when something difficult is also weighing on you. In Psalm 46, the invitation to stillness comes amid images of roaring waters and troubled nations. The passage does not wait for a peaceful setting before speaking of God.

That contrast makes room for a reflection: a pause does not have to resolve what is happening to be meaningful. Stillness can coexist with unfinished tasks and unanswered questions.

If you’d like to reflect, where could a brief pause fit into your day?

Make the reflection your own

Choose one question rather than rushing through all three. You might consider another person’s well-being without reopening an unsafe conversation, name what waiting feels like, or set aside a brief pause in an ordinary day. You can keep your answer private, write it down or simply sit with the question.
