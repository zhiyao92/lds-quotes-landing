Love and unfinished work

LDS Quotes reflection

Sometimes an ordinary day ends with something unresolved: a missed deadline, a tense conversation, a task left undone. Jeffrey R. Holland’s “Tomorrow the Lord Will Do Wonders among You” places God’s love beneath the work of improvement, rather than at its finish line. Read that way, accountability need not become a verdict on someone’s worth. The unfinished work can matter without being the measure of the person doing it.

If you’d like to reflect: what might a kinder, still-honest next step look like?

Room to keep learning

LDS Quotes reflection

After an awkward conversation, it can be hard to separate “I could have handled that better” from “I should have known better than to make any mistake.”

Jeffrey R. Holland’s “Be Ye Therefore Perfect—Eventually” names imperfection as part of the earthly journey. That distinction leaves room to take a mistake seriously without treating it as the whole story of a person. A clarification or apology can be worthwhile even when learning is still unfinished.

If you’d like to reflect, what could a manageable repair look like after a misunderstanding?

Kindness in a difficult week

LDS Quotes reflection

An ordinary errand can take extra effort during a difficult week. Henry B. Eyring’s words in “Mountains to Climb” leave room for blessing amid adversity without promising that faith will make hardship or illness immediately end.

A reflection on this passage: receiving something good does not require calling the whole situation good. Help with a task or a patient response can matter while the difficulty remains unresolved.

If you’d like, what small kindness could make an ordinary errand gentler?
